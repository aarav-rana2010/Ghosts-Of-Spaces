01 / THE MACHINE

A SMALL MACHINE.
A GIANT LEGACY.

NASA's Opportunity rover landed on Mars on January 25, 2004. It was part of the Mars Exploration Rover mission, alongside its twin rover, Spirit. Its primary mission was designed to last just 90 Martian days, known as sols.

But Opportunity kept going. It travelled across the Martian landscape, examined rocks, studied soil and discovered evidence that liquid water had once shaped the planet.

02 / ITS JOURNEY

EVERY MISSION HAS A TIMELINE.

JULY 7, 2003

Launch

Opportunity began its journey from Earth aboard a Delta II rocket.

JANUARY 25, 2004

Landing on Mars

The rover successfully landed at Meridiani Planum.

2004–2010

Discoveries

Opportunity investigated rocks and geological formations that revealed evidence of ancient water.

JUNE 2018

The dust storm

A planet-wide dust storm blocked sunlight, preventing the solar-powered rover from recharging properly.

FEBRUARY 13, 2019

Mission declared complete

After repeated attempts to contact the rover, NASA officially ended the mission.

03 / THE SCIENTIFIC LEGACY

WHAT DID IT LEAVE BEHIND?

Opportunity found geological evidence that ancient Mars had environments where liquid water was present. Its discoveries helped scientists understand the planet's past habitability.

THE FINAL CHAPTER

Opportunity's last communication was received on June 10, 2018. A massive dust storm had darkened the Martian sky and reduced the sunlight reaching its solar panels. NASA continued trying to contact it for months, but the rover never responded again.
