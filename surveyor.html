The Mission

The Robot That Met the Astronauts

Surveyor 3 was an uncrewed NASA lunar lander launched on April 17, 1967. It successfully landed in the Ocean of Storms on April 20, 1967. The spacecraft studied lunar soil and photographed the surface, helping prepare for future crewed Apollo landings.

Mission Timeline

From Earth to the Moon

April 17, 1967 — Launch

Surveyor 3 launched from Cape Kennedy aboard an Atlas-Centaur rocket.

April 20, 1967 — Lunar Landing

The spacecraft landed in the Ocean of Storms and began transmitting images.

1967 — Surface Experiments

Its surface sampler investigated the mechanical properties of lunar soil.

November 19, 1969 — Apollo 12

Astronauts Pete Conrad and Alan Bean landed nearby and visited Surveyor 3.

November 1969 — Historic Retrieval

The astronauts removed selected components, including the television camera, and returned them to Earth for examination.

Scientific Legacy

What Surveyor 3 Achieved

Lunar Soil Investigation

Its surface sampler helped scientists study the strength and physical properties of lunar soil.

Apollo 12 Visit

Apollo 12 astronauts examined the lander and retrieved components for scientific analysis back on Earth.

Preparing for Apollo

Surveyor missions provided valuable information about lunar landing conditions and surface materials.

Beyond the Mission

A Meeting Across Two Generations

Surveyor 3 became part of one of the most remarkable moments in space exploration: a robotic spacecraft landed on the Moon in 1967, and astronauts visited it in 1969. Its mission helped build confidence in the technology and knowledge needed for human lunar exploration.
