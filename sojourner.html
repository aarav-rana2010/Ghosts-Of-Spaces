SOJOURNER

Small in size. Massive in history. Sojourner became the first successful wheeled rover to operate on another planet, opening a new chapter in robotic exploration.

The Mission

The Little Rover That Made History

Sojourner was part of NASA's Mars Pathfinder mission. It landed on Mars on July 4, 1997, inside Ares Vallis. The rover examined nearby rocks and soil, tested robotic mobility, and demonstrated a new way to explore another planet.

Mission Timeline

From Earth to Ares Vallis

December 4, 1996 — Launch

Mars Pathfinder launched aboard a Delta II rocket.

July 4, 1997 — Landing

The Pathfinder lander touched down in Ares Vallis, carrying Sojourner to the Martian surface.

July 1997 — First Exploration

Sojourner began examining rocks and soil around its landing site.

August 1997 — Extended Operations

The rover continued its scientific work beyond its original planned mission duration.

September 27, 1997 — Final Contact

NASA received the final successful communication from the Pathfinder lander and Sojourner mission.

Scientific Legacy

What Sojourner Achieved

First Successful Mars Rover

Sojourner demonstrated that a small mobile robot could explore the Martian surface and investigate rocks.

Rock Investigations

Its instruments studied the composition of rocks and soil, helping scientists investigate the geological history of Mars.

A New Era of Exploration

The Pathfinder mission helped establish technologies and methods that influenced later Mars rover missions.

Beyond the Mission

Small Rover. Enormous Legacy.

Sojourner proved that robotic vehicles could travel across another planet, investigate its surface, and send valuable scientific information back to Earth. Its success helped pave the way for Spirit, Opportunity, Curiosity, and Perseverance.
