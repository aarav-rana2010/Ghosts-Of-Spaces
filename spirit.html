SPIRIT

A robotic explorer that transformed our understanding of Mars. Spirit travelled across an ancient Martian landscape, searching for evidence that liquid water once existed on the Red Planet.

The Mission

A Journey Across Ancient Mars

NASA's Spirit rover was one of two Mars Exploration Rovers launched in 2003. It landed inside Gusev Crater on January 4, 2004. Its mission was to study Martian rocks and soil, investigate the planet's geological history, and search for signs of past water.

Mission Timeline

Seven Years on the Red Planet

June 10, 2003 — Launch

Spirit began its journey from Earth aboard a Delta II rocket.

January 4, 2004 — Landing

Spirit successfully landed in Gusev Crater on Mars.

2005 — Columbia Hills

Spirit explored the Columbia Hills and discovered rocks showing evidence of past water-related activity.

2007 — Home Plate

The rover investigated an unusual geological feature called Home Plate.

April 2009 — Stuck in Sand

Spirit became trapped in soft Martian soil, limiting its mobility.

May 25, 2011 — Mission Ends

After repeated communication attempts, NASA officially concluded the mission.

Scientific Legacy

What Spirit Discovered

Evidence of Ancient Water

Spirit found minerals and geological features that provided evidence of past water-related processes on Mars.

The Columbia Hills

Its exploration revealed a diverse geological landscape and rocks altered by water.

A Lasting Legacy

Spirit helped establish that ancient Mars had environments much wetter and more complex than today's dry surface.

Beyond the Mission

A Rover That Changed Mars Exploration

Spirit was designed to operate for only 90 Martian days, but it continued exploring for more than six years. Its discoveries helped scientists understand Mars as a planet with a complex geological past and strengthened the search for ancient habitable environments.
