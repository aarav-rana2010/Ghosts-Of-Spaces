01 / THE MACHINE

A CAR BUILT FOR ANOTHER WORLD

The Lunar Roving Vehicle, commonly known as the LRV, was a lightweight, battery-powered vehicle developed for NASA's Apollo missions. It was designed to carry two astronauts across the Moon's challenging terrain.

Before the rover, astronauts had to explore the lunar surface on foot. The LRV dramatically expanded their range, allowing them to visit more geological locations, transport scientific equipment and collect lunar samples.

Built by Boeing, the rover had to be folded into a compact package inside the Lunar Module. Once on the Moon, astronauts unfolded and deployed it by hand.

02 / ENGINEERING

ENGINEERED FOR THE MOON

The LRV was not an ordinary electric car. Every major component had to work in a vacuum, under low gravity and across an unfamiliar, dusty landscape.

01 — POWER

Battery-Powered Mobility

Two 36-volt silver-zinc batteries supplied electrical power. The rover did not need fuel or an internal combustion engine.

02 — WHEELS

Wire-Mesh Wheels

Flexible wire-mesh tires with titanium tread elements provided traction on lunar soil without conventional rubber pneumatic tires.

03 — DRIVE

Four Electric Motors

Each wheel had its own electric drive motor, giving the rover four-wheel powered mobility.

04 — STEERING

Four-Wheel Steering

Separate front and rear steering systems helped the vehicle turn in the restricted space around the lunar landing sites.

05 — CONTROL

A Hand Controller

Astronauts operated the rover using a central hand controller rather than a conventional steering wheel.

06 — COMMUNICATION

Antennas and Cameras

Communication equipment and a television camera helped transmit information and images back to Earth.

03 / LUNAR JOURNEYS

THREE MISSIONS. THREE ROVERS.

The Lunar Roving Vehicle flew on the final three crewed Apollo lunar landing missions. Each mission used its rover to explore a different region of the Moon.

1971

Apollo 15 — The First Drive

Astronauts David Scott and James Irwin became the first humans to drive a vehicle on the Moon. Their rover explored the Hadley–Apennine region.

27.8 km TOTAL DISTANCE
1972

Apollo 16 — Exploring Descartes

John Young and Charles Duke used the rover to investigate the lunar highlands and collect geological samples.

26.7 km TOTAL DISTANCE
1972

Apollo 17 — The Final Journey

Eugene Cernan and Harrison Schmitt drove the final Apollo lunar rover in the Taurus–Littrow valley. It covered the greatest distance of the three missions.

35.9 km TOTAL DISTANCE

05 / THE LEGACY

THE WHEELS THAT CHANGED LUNAR EXPLORATION

The Lunar Roving Vehicle changed what astronauts could accomplish on the Moon. It helped them explore a wider area, carry more equipment and bring back valuable geological samples.

Although the Apollo programme ended, the engineering lessons from the LRV remain part of the story of planetary mobility and future lunar exploration.

"A small vehicle. A giant leap in exploration."
